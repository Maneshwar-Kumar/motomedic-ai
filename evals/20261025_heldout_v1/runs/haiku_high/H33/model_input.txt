- MotoMedic: The manual recommends shifting up from 2nd to 3rd gear at 30 km/h. Watch out: If the clutch is released abruptly at high acceleration, the motorcycle can move suddenly and the rider can lose control, which can lead to an accident, injury or loss of life to the rider or others, and damage to the motorcycle. Watch out: If the clutch is released abruptly without enough throttle, the motorcycle can have a rough start and the engine can stall.

## GEAR SHIFTING, RIDING AND STOPPING (pages 76-78)
[E1] The clutch lever must be fully depressed before attempting a gear shift. Failure to fully depress the clutch lever will cause a rough start or stalling of the engine besides causing damage to transmission parts.
[E2] When the vehicle is in neutral position, press gear shift lever down with toe to engage 1 st gear. GEAR SHIFT PATTERN
[E3] It is recommended to use half clutch condition when commuting in the city traffic condition alone (Engine RPM around idling to 2000 RPM) at 1st gear condition. In other gears and higher RPM avoid using half clutch which will proportionately reduce the clutch life. During acceleration / deceleration disengage the clutch completely, shift the gear and engage the clutch gradually (Not too slowly).
[E4] Recommended up shift speed Gear change: 1 st - 2 nd; km/h: 20
    Recommended up shift speed Gear change: 2 nd - 3 rd; km/h: 30
    Recommended up shift speed Gear change: 3 rd - 4 th; km/h: 40
    Recommended up shift speed Gear change: 4 th - 5 th; km/h: 60
    Recommended up shift speed Gear change: 5 th - 6 th; km/h: 75
[E5] Recommended down shift speed Gear change: 6 th - 5 th; km/h: 70
    Recommended down shift speed Gear change: 5 th - 4 th; km/h: 55
    Recommended down shift speed Gear change: 4 th - 3 rd; km/h: 35
    Recommended down shift speed Gear change: 3 rd - 2 nd; km/h: 25
    Recommended down shift speed Gear change: 2 nd - 1 st; km/h: 18
[E6] (CAUTION) If the clutch lever is released abruptly and throttle opening is done insufficiently the motorcycle will have a rough start and cause the engine to stall.
[E7] (CAUTION) If the acceleration is very high and clutch lever is released abruptly, it will cause motorcycle to move suddenly, which will lead to loss of control leading to an accident resulting in injury and or loss of life to rider / other road users / passed by, besides damage to the motorcycle.
[E8] (CAUTION) Always exercise utmost caution while releasing clutch
[E9] (CAUTION) and riding the motorcycle.
[E10] (CAUTION) Follow the recommended up shifting and down shifting speeds.
[E11] (NOTE) Always start motorcycle with the gear in neutral condition.
[E12] (NOTE) Always move the motorcycle in first gear position only.
[E13] (NOTE) When the engine speed decreases or while climbing a gradient or running at a a reduced speed, shift to the appropriate lower gear to prevent the engine from stalling or straining to pull.
[E14] (NOTE) The clutch lever must be fully depressed, throttle modulated and immediately shifted to next gear as per recommended gear shifting speeds.
[E15] (NOTE) Failure to follow the above may cause a rough start or stalling or improper gear shifting of the engine besides causing damage to transmission parts.
[E16] (NOTE) Always shift gears to neutral position just before bringing the vehicle to a complete stop.
[E17] Close throttle fully and release the clutch lever slowly ensuring the motorcycle is in neutral position and neutral lamp is glowing.
[E18] Stop the motorcycle in a safe place, turn OFF ignition and switch OFF engine kill switch.
[E19] While riding on steep slopes and descending long roads it is recommended to use the brake intermittently along with Engine braking in a combination way to achieve effective braking. Engine braking is achieved by performing downshifting of the gear from the current gear to slow down the vehicle.
