RIDER QUESTION: When do the brake pads need replacement?

EARLIER CONVERSATION: none

MANUAL SECTIONS:

## BRAKE PADS (pages 90-91)
[E1] The front and rear brake pads must be checked for wear at the intervals specified in the periodic maintenance chart.
[E2] Front/Rear : The pads need to be replaced if a brake pad is warm to the indicator.
[E3] (NOTE) Royal Enfield Motorcycles fitted with disc brakes have the optimum braking systems and are built to give a superior and safer braking performance under all conditions.
[E4] (NOTE) Disc braking systems can produce a mild noise under certain riding conditions. This is absolutely normal and characteristic of the disc brake pads across the motorcycle industry. This in no way will affect the performance of the motorcycle or the braking system.
[E5] (NOTE) At Royal Enfield, we have robust and rigorous testing and development protocols and adhere to global validation standards of quality and durability. We are committed to giving our customers the best possible ownership experience with our motorcycles.

## FRONT WHEEL REMOVAL (pages 91-93)
[E6] Ensure motorcycle is upright on a firm and flat surface.
[E7] Locate a scissor jack under the engine and lift motorcycle such that the front wheel is off the ground by minimum 15 cm.
[E8] Loosen and remove fork clamp bolt 2 Nos from LHS front fork assembly by using 6 mm Allen key available in the tool kit.
[E9] Loosen and remove spindle bolt from LHS front fork assembly by using front spindle adapter, ring spanner and extension tube are available in the tool kit.
[E10] Gently pull out the spindle from LHS by hand once fully unscrewed.
[E11] Gently lower the wheel such that it comes out of the fork legs.
[E12] Remove spacers from front wheel hub on both LHS and RHS.
[E13] (CAUTION) Take care to secure the wheel spacers and speed sensor while removing the axle from the forks.
[E14] Do not press the brake lever when front wheel is removed as this will result in the brake pads coming too far out of the brake caliper.

## REAR WHEEL REMOVAL (pages 95-98)
[E15] Ensure motorcycle is upright on a firm and flat surface.
[E16] Apply the center stand.
[E17] Observe and mark the alignment indexes on both chain adjuster on left and right side swing arm.
[E18] Loosen the lock nuts and adjuster nuts fully on the left and right side chain adjuster by using 24 mm ring spanner available in the tool kit.
[E19] Hold wheel spindle on left side firmly and loosen hex nut on right side.
[E20] Remove the nut and washer from the wheel spindle.
[E21] Push rear wheel fully into the swing arm.
[E22] Support rear wheel from bottom and pull out wheel spindle from the left side swing arm.
[E23] Release the brake hose gently and remove caliper assembly from swing arm on right side.
[E24] (CAUTION) Ensure brake hose does not get damaged or kinked while removing. Support caliper assembly suitably and away from swing arm.
[E25] (CAUTION) Release the chain from the sprocket and ensure it does not get jammed or damaged when removing rear wheel.
[E26] (CAUTION) Remove support from wheel bottom and gently slide out rear wheel from the swing arm with rear sprocket, brake disc and spacers.
[E27] (CAUTION) Do not press the rear brake pedal when the rear wheel is being removed as this will cause the brake pads to dislocate from the brake caliper.
[E28] (CAUTION) Place a 4 mm thick wooden piece or cardboard sheet between the brake pads to avoid activation of brake pads if rear brake pedal is accidentally pressed.
[E29] (CAUTION) Do not pull up the rear brake pedal to lift or raise the motorcycle for any reason.
